Privacy Policy

Effective November 14, 2026

Hearth does not have an account system, a server, or analytics. Everything you enter stays in the app's private storage on your device. We cannot see it, and we have no way to recover it for you.

What Hearth collects

Nothing. Hearth does not collect, transmit, sell, rent, or share personal information. The app does not request the Android internet permission, so it cannot send data off the device.

Where your data lives

Tasks, calendar entries, notes, food inventory, recipes, meals, medicines, health entries, kids' records, supplies, and settings are stored in an app-private file on your device. Android prevents other apps from reading this file. Uninstalling Hearth deletes it.

Backups and exports

You can create a backup from Settings → Data → Export a backup. The app writes a JSON file to the location you choose using Android's system file picker. That file is under your control; if you save it to a cloud drive, that provider's terms apply. Hearth does not upload it anywhere.

You can restore a backup from Settings → Data → Import a backup, and delete all records with Settings → Data → Delete everything.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily check-in, Hearth asks for the Android notification permission and schedules a local alarm. Event reminders are scheduled the same way, 30 minutes before the event. Notification text is generated on your device from your own records. Hearth uses the "run at startup" permission only to restore those local reminders after a reboot.

Health and children's data

Health, medicine, growth, homework, and baby-care entries are personal records that you choose to store. They are not medical advice and are never transmitted. Hearth is intended for adults managing a household. We do not knowingly collect information from children — the app collects no information from anyone.

Third parties

Hearth contains no advertising SDKs, no analytics SDKs, no crash reporters, and no social integrations. It is built with open-source Android libraries (AndroidX, Jetpack Compose, Kotlin, kotlinx.serialization) that run entirely on the device.

Your choices

  • Edit or delete any record inside the app at any time.
  • Export a copy of your data.
  • Delete everything from Settings → Data → Delete everything.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all local data.

Changes

If the app's data practices change (for example, if a future version adds optional cloud sync), this policy will be updated before that feature ships and the effective date will change.
